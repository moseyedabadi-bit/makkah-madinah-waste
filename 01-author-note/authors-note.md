# Author’s Note

In the name of God, the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate.

On the 16th of December, corresponding to the 25th of Jumada al-Thani,
I entered the Sacred Mosque in the state of Ihram to perform a proxy Tawaf,
accompanying two elderly pilgrims.

Our formation within the crowd was deliberate:
the two elders walking side by side ahead,
and I walking behind them to offer support and protection.

The first round passed peacefully.

At the beginning of the second round,
a small broken piece of candy — sharp like glass —
pierced the sole of my foot.

The pain was sudden and deep,
yet the movement of the pilgrims did not allow stopping.

In that moment, a verse came to my heart:

> “Not a leaf falls but that He knows it.”  
> *(Qur’an 6:59)*

I knew then that nothing in that place is accidental.

Later, along the path, I found a single lost sock.
A simple object — insignificant at first glance —
yet meaningful in a place where millions walk barefoot in humility.

It reminded me that every abandoned item,
no matter how small,
can become harm to another servant of God.

From that moment, a question formed quietly within me:

If cleanliness is part of faith,
and if harm to others is forbidden,
then responsibility within the Holy Sites
cannot be only an operational duty —
it must also be a shared moral awareness.

This framework did not emerge from policy discussions,
nor from institutional planning.

It emerged from walking,
from pain,
from observation,
and from reflection.

I present this work not as instruction,
but as contemplation.

Not as authority,
but as responsibility.

If these pages contribute, even slightly,
to protecting the dignity of pilgrims
and preserving the sanctity of the Holy Sites,
then that is sufficient.

And God knows best.
