# Page 06 – Economic & Incentive-Based Model

## Purpose

This section explores non-monetary and dignity-based incentive mechanisms
that may encourage responsible waste behavior
within pilgrimage environments.

It does not propose financial rewards,
commercial transactions, or material compensation.

Its focus is motivation, not payment.

---

## Context

Behavioral studies consistently show that
positive reinforcement is more effective than punishment,
especially in temporary, emotionally intensive environments
such as pilgrimage.

However, direct financial incentives
may introduce unintended consequences,
including inequality, exploitation, or distortion of intention.

Therefore, incentive design must remain ethical,
symbolic, and collective.

---

## Conceptual Incentive Philosophy

This framework adopts the principle that:

Motivation should reinforce dignity,
not replace intention.

Incentives are viewed as:

- Acknowledgment, not reward
- Recognition, not payment
- Encouragement, not entitlement

This distinction preserves sincerity of worship.

---

## Collective-Based Incentives

Rather than targeting individuals,
this model emphasizes collective-level incentives.

Recognition may occur at levels such as:

- Caravan groups
- National delegations
- Organized pilgrimage units

This reduces individual competition
and strengthens shared identity.
