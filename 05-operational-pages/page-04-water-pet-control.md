# Page 04 – Water PET Control & Responsibility Framework

## Purpose

This section addresses one of the most critical sources of waste
within pilgrimage environments:
single-use plastic water bottles (PET).

The objective is not to restrict access to water,
but to establish responsibility mechanisms
around its distribution, consumption, and recovery.

---

## Context

In the climate conditions of Makkah and Madinah,
drinking water is a vital necessity.

Millions of bottles are consumed daily during Hajj and Umrah,
making water packaging the dominant contributor
to plastic waste within the Holy Sites.

The issue is therefore not the presence of water,
but the absence of structured responsibility
after consumption.

---

## Conceptual Challenge

Water is a life-sustaining good,
yet its packaging becomes a source of harm
when discarded improperly.

This creates a dual obligation:

- Ensuring unrestricted access to drinking water
- Preventing environmental and physical harm
  caused by empty bottles

Both obligations must coexist.

---

## Responsibility-Based Control Approach

This framework proposes shifting
from individual blame
to organizational responsibility.

Rather than monitoring individual pilgrims,
responsibility is conceptually assigned
to the entities that organize and supply pilgrims.

These may include:

- Caravan operators
- National pilgrimage organizations
- Group coordinators

This approach aligns responsibility
with organizational capacity.

---

## Purchase–Recovery Alignment

Within this conceptual model,
the quantity of water distributed
and the quantity of empty PET bottles recovered
can be observed at an aggregate level.

The objective is not exact matching,
but behavioral awareness and accountability.

This alignment encourages:

- Organized collection behavior
- Internal group coordination
- Reduced random littering

---

## Incentive-Based Motivation

Instead of penalties,
positive incentives may be conceptually introduced
to encourage higher recovery rates.

Recognition may be framed as:

- Symbolic acknowledgment
- Sustainability-based distinction
- Collective appreciation

Such mechanisms remain policy-level concepts,
subject to institutional design.

---

## Role of Authorities

This framework does not propose operational control
by religious or security authorities.

It recognizes that regulatory bodies
retain full discretion over implementation,
oversight, and enforcement mechanisms.

The model contributes only a conceptual lens
for aligning consumption with responsibility.

---

## Expected Ethical Impact

Through structured responsibility around water packaging,
this approach aims to:

- Reduce plastic litter in sacred spaces
- Improve safety for barefoot pilgrims
- Support environmental sustainability
- Reinforce collective accountability
- Preserve dignity without restriction

---

## Closing Reflection

Water sustains life.

Responsibility sustains dignity.

When both are honored together,
necessity does not become negligence.
